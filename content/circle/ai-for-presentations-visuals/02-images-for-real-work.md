# Images for Real Work

Most tutorials about AI images are about making art — astronauts riding horses, dragons in cafes, that kind of thing. This lesson is not that. This is about the boring, useful images you actually need: an illustration for a handout, a header for a flyer, a square graphic for a social post, a poster for a classroom wall.

Getting one of these right in 5 minutes is a real skill. Getting it wrong wastes an hour and the image still looks weird.

---

## The tools we'll use

- [https://www.midjourney.com](https://www.midjourney.com) — best quality, but paid only. Worth it if you make images weekly.
- [https://openai.com/dall-e-3](https://openai.com/dall-e-3) — built into ChatGPT. Easy starting point.
- [https://gemini.google.com](https://gemini.google.com) — Google's Gemini includes the "Nano Banana" image model. Surprisingly good for edits ("change the shirt to blue") and for putting a real product into a scene.
- [https://ideogram.ai](https://ideogram.ai) — the only one that handles **text inside images** well. Use it for posters with words on them.
- [https://www.canva.com](https://www.canva.com) — has built-in AI image generation. Convenient if you're already designing there.

Start with DALL-E inside ChatGPT or Ideogram for text. Add Midjourney later if you need it.

---

## Step 1: A good prompt has five parts

The biggest mistake beginners make is one-word prompts like "happy kids." You get garbage.

A working image prompt looks like this:

```
[subject] + [setting/context] + [style] + [composition] + [mood/lighting]
```

Copy this template:

```
A [subject doing something specific] in [setting]. Style: [flat illustration / photograph / watercolor / line drawing]. Composition: [close-up / wide shot / centered / overhead]. Mood: [warm and natural / clean and minimal / bright and playful]. No text in the image.
```

That last line — "No text in the image" — saves you from the worst AI behavior, which is sprinkling fake misspelled words into your picture.

---

## Step 2: Three flavors, one prompt template

Same structure, three completely different images you might actually need:

- **Teacher:** *"A child reading a book at a small wooden desk in a sunny classroom. Style: warm flat illustration. Composition: medium shot from the side. Mood: calm and focused. No text in the image."*
- **NGO worker:** *"A group of three women filling water containers at a community well in a rural village. Style: documentary photograph. Composition: wide shot, natural framing. Mood: golden hour, hopeful but real. No text in the image."*
- **Business:** *"A clean isometric illustration of a small warehouse with shelves and a forklift. Style: flat vector, two-color palette of navy blue and orange. Composition: 3D isometric angle. Mood: clean and professional. No text in the image."*

The teacher's image goes on a worksheet. The NGO's image goes in a donor email. The business image goes on the cover of a process document. Each needs a different style — pick yours on purpose, not by default.

---

## Step 3: Text in images — only Ideogram

Every other tool will get the words wrong. They will hallucinate letters, double letters, misspell your own organization's name. This is not a problem you can prompt your way out of in DALL-E or Midjourney.

For anything with real words — a poster, a flyer header, a quote graphic — use [ideogram.ai](https://ideogram.ai).

```
A poster with the text "Parent Evening — Thursday 6pm" in bold sans-serif at the top. Below: a warm illustration of a parent and child reading together. Background: soft cream. Style: friendly, modern, clean.
```

Even Ideogram fails sometimes. Always zoom in and read every letter before you use the image. Always.

---

## Step 4: Style consistency across a set

If you need 4 images for a deck or a handout, the worst thing you can do is generate them one at a time with no plan. Each one will have a different style and the whole thing will look like a ransom note.

Pin down a style, then reuse it word-for-word:

```
Style reference: flat illustration, two-color palette (navy and warm orange), thick line work, simple shapes, white background, no shadows.
```

Paste that line into every image prompt for that project. Same style, same colors, same look. The set will feel like one thing instead of five things.

For more control, Midjourney has `--sref` (style references from an image). Worth learning if you're paying for it.

---

## Step 5: When to skip AI and use stock photos

AI is not always the right answer. Use stock photos when:

- You need **real-looking humans** in a photograph. AI faces still look slightly off, and people notice.
- You need a **specific place** — a real city, a recognizable landmark, a specific product.
- The image needs to be **diverse and respectful** in a way you can verify. AI defaults can be biased and weird.
- You're in a hurry. A good stock photo in 30 seconds beats a wonky AI image in 20 minutes.

Free stock photo sites worth knowing:

- [https://unsplash.com](https://unsplash.com)
- [https://www.pexels.com](https://www.pexels.com)
- [https://pixabay.com](https://pixabay.com)

For NGO work especially, [https://nappy.co](https://nappy.co) and [https://www.disabilityin.org/disability-images](https://www.disabilityin.org/disability-images) have stock images that aren't all corporate white guys in suits.

## What AI images still get wrong

Be honest about this with yourself and your audience.

- **Hands.** Six fingers. Three fingers. Hands going into other hands. Check every hand.
- **Text.** Already covered, but worth repeating. Fake words everywhere.
- **Faces of real people.** Don't try to generate "a photo of my colleague Sara." It won't be Sara.
- **Brand logos and uniforms.** If a school uniform or a company logo appears, it will be wrong. Either crop it out or photograph the real thing.
- **Cultural specifics.** "A village in Lebanon" might look like a generic Mediterranean stock cliché. Be specific or use a real photo.
- **Group photos.** The more people in the image, the more weird limbs and faces you get.

Rule of thumb: **AI is great for illustrations, decent for backgrounds, risky for photos of people.** When in doubt, lean illustration or use stock.

## Homework

1. Write one prompt using the Step 1 template for an image you actually need this month. Generate it in DALL-E (inside ChatGPT) or [ideogram.ai](https://ideogram.ai). Don't accept the first one — generate it twice.
2. Pick a style sentence (like the one in Step 4). Use it to generate 3 images that should look like a set. Put them side by side. Do they feel like siblings or strangers?
3. Try one image with text in [ideogram.ai](https://ideogram.ai). Zoom in. Find at least one thing wrong with it.
4. Find one stock photo on [unsplash.com](https://unsplash.com) or [pexels.com](https://www.pexels.com) that's better than what AI gave you. Save it for next time.
