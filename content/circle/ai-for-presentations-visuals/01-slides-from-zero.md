# Slides from Zero

Almost nobody enjoys making slides. You sit down to make a 10-slide deck and three hours later you're still picking fonts. The good news: AI is genuinely useful here. It will not make a *great* deck for you, but it will get you from a blank screen to a rough draft in about five minutes — and the rough draft is the part everyone hates.

This lesson is about going from a bullet outline to a working deck, and then editing it so it doesn't look like everyone else's AI deck.

---

## The tools we'll use

- [https://gamma.app](https://gamma.app) — generates a full deck from a prompt or outline. Free tier is enough.
- [https://tome.app](https://tome.app) — similar idea, slightly different style. Worth trying once.
- [https://claude.ai](https://claude.ai) or [https://chatgpt.com](https://chatgpt.com) — for writing the outline and rewriting slide text.
- [https://www.canva.com](https://www.canva.com) — for editing and polishing the result.

Pick Gamma for now. It's the most forgiving for beginners and the output is the least cringe.

---

## Step 1: Write the outline before you touch a slide tool

This is the step everyone skips. Don't.

A slide tool turns text into slides. If your text is vague, your slides will be vague. Spend five minutes on the outline in Claude or ChatGPT first.

Copy this prompt:

```
I'm preparing a [X minute] talk for [audience]. The goal is for them to [one specific outcome].

Help me build an outline with:
- An opener (a question, a number, or a short story)
- 3 to 5 body sections, each with one main point
- A close that tells them what to do next

Keep each bullet short. No filler. Ask me 2 questions before you write anything.

Topic: [your topic]
```

The "ask me 2 questions" line is important. It forces the AI to figure out what it doesn't know instead of inventing it.

---

## Step 2: Three flavors of the same move

Same prompt, totally different decks:

- **Teacher:** a 15-minute parent evening talk on "How we teach reading in Year 3." Goal: parents leave knowing how to support reading at home.
- **NGO worker:** a 10-minute donor pitch for a new after-school program. Goal: a specific donor commits to funding one classroom.
- **Business:** a 20-minute internal presentation proposing a switch to a new supplier. Goal: the leadership team approves a 3-month pilot.

For each, the opener, body, and close are completely different. The teacher opens with a story about one child. The NGO opens with a number ("47 kids on our waiting list"). The business opens with the cost problem. Don't let AI pick the opener — you pick it, based on what your audience actually responds to.

---

## Step 3: Generate the deck

Take your outline into Gamma:

1. Go to [gamma.app](https://gamma.app), click "Create new" → "Generate."
2. Paste your outline. Set the number of slides to match (usually 6–12).
3. Pick a theme. Avoid the flashy ones for work. The plain ones look more professional.
4. Hit generate.

You'll have a deck in about 30 seconds. It will not be the final deck. It will be 70% there.

---

## Step 4: Edit it so it doesn't look like an AI deck

This is the part that separates a deck people remember from a deck they tune out.

What AI decks always get wrong:

- **Too many words on each slide.** A slide is not a document. Cut every slide to 6 words or fewer where possible. Move details to your speaker notes.
- **Generic stock images.** The default images are smiling office people in suits. They make your deck look like a 2012 LinkedIn post. Replace them — either with your own photos, or with images you generate yourself (next lesson).
- **The same layout on every slide.** Vary it. One slide with a big number. One with just a quote. One with a single image and three words.
- **Bullet points everywhere.** Some slides shouldn't have bullets at all. A single sentence in big text is often stronger.

Rewrite the slide text yourself, or paste it back into Claude:

```
Here is the text from one slide. Rewrite it as the shortest possible version that still makes the point. Maximum 8 words on the slide. If something needs more explanation, put it in speaker notes.

Slide text:
[paste]
```

---

## Step 5: Speaker notes are where the real talk lives

The slides are not your script. The speaker notes are. Most people forget this.

Ask AI to draft the notes:

```
For each slide of this deck, write 2–3 sentences of speaker notes — what I should actually say out loud. Conversational, not formal. No "as you can see on this slide."

[paste your outline or the slide text]
```

Read the notes out loud. If a sentence feels weird in your mouth, change it. Your voice has to match the words.

---

## What AI gets wrong with slides

- **It will pad your deck.** If you ask for 10 slides, it gives you 10 — even if your content only needs 6. Cut ruthlessly.
- **The themes look similar.** Everyone using Gamma right now has decks that look vaguely the same. A bit of manual polish in Canva goes a long way.
- **It can't read the room.** Donor pitches, classrooms, and boardrooms each have a different rhythm. You know that rhythm. The AI doesn't.
- **Auto-generated charts are often wrong.** If there are real numbers, build the chart yourself.

Rule of thumb: **AI builds the skeleton. You add the muscle.** The opener, the close, and the one slide you really care about — those should all be touched by your hand.

## Homework

1. Pick one talk or pitch you'll actually give in the next month. Use the Step 1 prompt in Claude or ChatGPT to build the outline. Save it.
2. Take that outline into [gamma.app](https://gamma.app) and generate a deck. Don't share it yet. Look at it honestly.
3. Cut the deck by at least one slide. Rewrite three slides to use 8 or fewer words. Replace one generic stock image with something better (your own photo is fine).
4. Generate speaker notes with the Step 5 prompt. Read them out loud once. Change anything that feels stiff.
