# Learning a New Skill with AI

Most of us already use AI as an assistant — write this email, summarize that PDF, fix this bug. This module is about something different: using AI as a **coach**. The shift in posture is the whole lesson.

A coach pushes you. An assistant does the thing for you. The first one grows you. The second one doesn't.

We start with the friendliest version: learning a new skill.

---

## The Tutor That Never Gets Tired

Before AI, learning a new skill on your own meant one of three things: buy a book, watch YouTube, or pay for a course. Each has a problem. The book doesn't know what you already understand. The video can't answer your specific question. The course moves at the pace of the slowest student.

A good AI conversation fixes all three. It meets you where you are, answers the exact question you have, and waits as long as you need.

The catch: it only works if you actually treat it like a tutor. If you ask vague questions, you get vague answers. If you stop after one prompt, you got a search result, not a lesson.

Tools we'll use:

- [claude.ai](https://claude.ai) — best for long, thoughtful explanations
- [chatgpt.com](https://chatgpt.com) — best for back-and-forth and voice mode
- [gemini.google.com](https://gemini.google.com) — best when you want to pull in YouTube videos and the web

## Demo: Learning Conversational Spanish in 30 Days

We'll use one example the whole lesson: you want to hold a basic conversation in Spanish in 30 days. Pick your own skill later — Excel, public speaking, basic Python, whatever. The pattern is the same.

### Step 1: Get a curriculum, not just topics

A bad prompt: "Teach me Spanish."

A better prompt — paste this in:

```
I want to be able to hold a 5-minute casual conversation in Spanish
in 30 days. I have 30 minutes a day, 6 days a week.

I already know about 50 basic words from school but I can't form sentences.

Design a 30-day curriculum. For each week, tell me:
- the main goal
- the grammar concepts to focus on
- the vocabulary themes
- a daily 30-minute breakdown (study / practice / review)

Be realistic. If 30 days isn't enough to truly converse, say so
and tell me what level I'll actually reach.
```

Notice what makes this work: you gave it your starting level, your time budget, your real goal, and you asked it to push back if the goal is unrealistic.

---

### Step 2: Ask for explanations at the right level

The single most underused trick in AI learning is telling it what level to explain at. Same concept, three different answers:

```
Explain the Spanish subjunctive like I'm a complete beginner.
Use a simple analogy. No grammar jargon.
```

```
Explain the Spanish subjunctive like I'm an intermediate learner.
I know the present and past tense already. Use examples.
```

```
Explain the Spanish subjunctive like I'm preparing for the DELE B2 exam.
Cover the trigger phrases and the most common mistakes.
```

If the first explanation doesn't land, don't reread it. Say "explain it differently, I didn't get the part about X." That's the move.

---

### Step 3: Quiz yourself, don't just read

Reading an AI answer feels like learning. It usually isn't. The retention happens when you have to produce the answer yourself.

```
Quiz me on the 20 most common Spanish verbs in the present tense.
Ask me one at a time. Wait for my answer before giving the next.
When I get one wrong, explain why, then come back to it in 3 questions.
```

This single prompt is worth more than 10 vocabulary lists. The spaced repetition — coming back to the wrong ones — is what makes it stick.

---

### Step 4: Spaced practice across days

```
I learned these 15 Spanish phrases yesterday: [paste list].
Build me a 10-minute review session that mixes these with the
20 phrases I learned last week. Throw in 3 new ones at the end.
```

This is how language apps like Anki work under the hood. You don't need the app. You need the discipline to actually do the review.

## When to Ask AI vs Read a Book vs Take a Real Course

Be honest about what AI can't do.

**Ask AI when:** you have a specific question, you're stuck on a concept, you want to be quizzed, you want a custom plan, or you want to practice (writing, speaking via voice mode).

**Read a book when:** the topic has depth that needs a long arc — history, philosophy, a single expert's worldview. AI summaries flatten these. A book by a great teacher beats an AI conversation about that teacher.

**Take a real course when:** you need accountability, feedback on physical skill (pronunciation, technique), credentials, or a community of fellow learners. AI doesn't care if you quit. A human teacher does, and a classmate definitely does.

The honest truth: most of us quit when learning alone. AI removes the "I don't understand" excuse but it doesn't remove the "I didn't show up today" excuse. That's still on you.

---

## What AI Won't Do for You

It won't know what you already know unless you tell it. It won't notice you've been faking comprehension. It won't call you out for skipping practice for a week. It won't catch a deep misunderstanding that you're confidently building on. And it confidently makes things up — especially with niche topics, foreign languages, and specific factual claims. Verify anything that matters.

## Homework

1. Pick **one** skill you actually want to learn in the next 30-90 days. Use the curriculum prompt above on [claude.ai](https://claude.ai) to get a real plan. Save the conversation.

2. Take the hardest concept from week 1 of your curriculum. Ask AI to explain it three ways: like a beginner, like an intermediate, like an expert. Notice which one actually clicks.

3. Use the quiz prompt to test yourself on something you "learned" this week. See how much you actually remember without looking.

4. Be honest with the group next session: did you do the work, or did you just read AI's answers and feel productive? The difference matters.
