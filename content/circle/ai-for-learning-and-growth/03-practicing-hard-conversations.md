# Practicing Hard Conversations

The conversations that matter most in a career are usually the ones we walk into cold. The raise ask. The disagreement with a boss. The negotiation. The hard feedback. The pitch.

We rehearse these in the shower, in the car, in the 30 seconds before we open the door. We almost never rehearse them out loud, with another person, where we can hear how we actually sound.

AI doesn't fix this completely. But it gets you 70% of the way there, and that's a lot more than 0%.

---

## Why Role-Play With AI Works Better Than You'd Think

Two reasons.

First, you can do it as many times as you want. You can blow the conversation up, restart, try a different angle, see how it lands. The first time you ask for a raise out loud should not be in front of your actual boss.

Second, AI is good at playing a character if you brief it well. Not perfect — it won't nail tone, it won't know your specific person, and it slides back into being agreeable if you don't keep pressing. But the basic shape of a tough counterparty? It can do that.

Tools we'll use:

- [chatgpt.com](https://chatgpt.com) — best because of voice mode; you can actually *speak* the conversation
- [claude.ai](https://claude.ai) — best for written role-play, holds character longer
- [gemini.google.com](https://gemini.google.com) — also has voice; worth trying

If your phone has the ChatGPT app, voice mode changes this lesson. You can practice while walking, in the car, anywhere private. The act of speaking out loud is half the value.

## Demo: Asking for a Raise

One example for the whole lesson: you want to ask your boss for a 15% raise. You've been in the role 18 months. You don't know if you deserve it. You don't know how she'll respond.

### The setup prompt

This is the most important part. A weak setup gets you a weak partner. Paste this and adapt it:

```
I want to practice a real conversation. You're going to play my boss.

Context about my boss:
- She's been in her role 4 years, runs a team of 8
- She's fair but direct, doesn't sugarcoat
- She controls the budget but has to justify raises to her own boss
- She likes me but isn't sentimental about it
- Last review she said I was "doing well, room to grow"

Context about me:
- I've been in this role 18 months
- I took on two extra responsibilities this year that weren't in my job description
- I haven't had a raise since I started
- I'm asking for 15%

Now: play her. Be realistic. Push back where a real boss would push back.
Don't go easy on me. Don't make it dramatic either — keep it like
a real Monday afternoon conversation.

I'll start by knocking on the door.
```

Notice what makes this work:

- You gave it real character details, not just "play my boss."
- You named the dynamic (fair but direct).
- You explicitly said "don't go easy" *and* "don't make it dramatic" — the two failure modes.
- You set the scene with an opening action.

---

### Running the conversation

Just go. Knock on the door. Say what you'd actually say. See what comes back.

When the response is too soft or too easy:

```
That was too agreeable. A real boss in this situation would push
back harder on [the specific thing]. Try again, tougher.
```

When the response is unrealistic in the other direction:

```
That was too aggressive — she wouldn't actually attack me like that.
Dial it back to "skeptical but professional."
```

You're directing. AI is acting. Keep adjusting until the texture feels real.

---

### The debrief prompt

After you've run the conversation 2-3 times, switch posture:

```
Drop the role-play. Now be my coach.

Looking back at the conversation we just had:
- What were the three weakest moments in what I said?
- Where did I undermine my own ask with hedging language?
- What's the one thing I should have led with but didn't?
- If she had pushed back harder on [specific objection], how would you recommend I respond?
```

This is where the real growth happens. The role-play exposes the problems. The debrief teaches you what to fix.

## Other Conversations Worth Practicing

The pattern is the same. The setup prompt changes:

**Giving hard feedback to a peer:**

```
Play a colleague I have to give critical feedback to.
He's defensive when challenged and tends to deflect by talking
about how busy he's been. The feedback: [paste it].
```

**Negotiating a contract or fee:**

```
Play a client who wants me to drop my price by 20%. He'll start
friendly, then get firm, then try a "take it or leave it" close.
I want to hold my price but not lose the deal.
```

**A pitch:**

```
Play a skeptical investor / donor / decision-maker who has heard
50 pitches like mine this year. Be polite but ask the hard questions
in the first two minutes. Cut me off when I ramble.
```

**Firing someone or ending a relationship:**

```
Play someone I have to let go after 2 years. He didn't see it coming.
React the way a real person would — shock, then anger, then bargaining.
I need to stay clear and kind without backing off the decision.
```

## Asking AI to Be Tougher

If the practice partner is too gentle (the default), use these:

```
Be tougher. A real version of this person would not let me get away with [the specific weak thing I said].
```

```
You're playing the version of this person on a bad day.
The pressure on her is real. She doesn't have time for fluff.
```

```
Play the skeptic. Assume my proposal is wrong until I prove otherwise.
```

Conversely, if it gets cartoonishly mean, dial back:

```
Less dramatic. Make it feel like a normal Tuesday, not a movie scene.
```

## What This Won't Do

It won't get tone right. AI doesn't know your boss's specific verbal tics, the way she pauses, the look she gives. It might write her dialogue too articulately. Real people stumble, repeat, half-finish thoughts.

It doesn't know your specific person. If you've worked with this human for 5 years, you know things AI can't guess.

It won't catch your body language. The raise conversation is half physical — how you sit, where you look, whether your voice shakes. AI sees none of this. If you have a trusted friend, do the final dry run with them.

It can get stuck in the role-play and start agreeing with you anyway. Notice when that happens and reset.

## Homework

1. Pick one real conversation you're avoiding right now. Write the setup prompt for it. Run the role-play at least three times — each time, change one thing about how you open.

2. After the role-plays, run the debrief prompt. Write down the three concrete changes you'll make in the real conversation.

3. Try voice mode on [chatgpt.com](https://chatgpt.com) or the app at least once. Speaking out loud is different from typing. You'll notice things about your own voice you can't notice on paper.

4. Optional but recommended: do the final dry run with one of us. AI got you 70%. A real human in the room with you gets you the last 30%.
