# Emails and Replies That Don't Sound Robotic

Most of the writing you do at work is small. A reply to a parent. A note to a donor. A follow-up to a client who's gone quiet. AI is genuinely good at this — but only if you stop it from sounding like AI.

The default voice of every chatbot is the same: warm, slightly formal, full of phrases like *"I hope this email finds you well"* and *"please don't hesitate to reach out."* Nobody talks like that. The whole module is about getting past that default.

---

## The two tools we'll use

- [https://claude.ai](https://claude.ai) — better for longer, more natural-sounding writing
- [https://chatgpt.com](https://chatgpt.com) — faster, good for short replies and brainstorming

Both are free to start. Pick one and stick with it for a week. Switching constantly will confuse you about what's actually working.

---

## Step 1: Teach the AI your voice

This is the single most important thing in this lesson. Before you ask for any email, paste in 2–3 real emails you wrote yourself, then say: *"This is how I write. Match this tone."*

Copy this prompt:

```
Here are three emails I wrote recently. Read them carefully and describe my writing style in 5 bullet points — sentence length, formality, common phrases I use, how I open and close, what I avoid. I will use this description later when I ask you to draft emails for me.

[paste email 1]
[paste email 2]
[paste email 3]
```

Save the 5-bullet description somewhere. That's your style guide. From now on, paste it at the top of any email request.

---

## Step 2: Short note → full email

This is the move that saves the most time. You type a rough sentence; AI turns it into the actual email.

```
Here is my writing style:
[paste your 5-bullet style guide]

Turn this rough note into a polished email. Keep it short. Do not add filler. Do not use the phrase "I hope this finds you well" or "please don't hesitate."

Rough note:
[your note here]
```

**Example rough note:**

*"Follow up with the supplier — third time asking about the March delivery date, polite but firm, I need an answer by Friday."*

That takes 10 seconds to type and produces a usable draft in another 10 seconds. The same move works for any short email you've been putting off — a reply to a parent, a thank-you to a donor, a chase to a client who's gone quiet.

---

## Step 3: The polite refusal

Saying no is the email everyone procrastinates on. AI is excellent here — it gives you words for things you'd rather not write.

```
Write a short, warm refusal. Do not over-apologise. Do not offer alternatives unless I told you to. End cleanly.

Situation: [describe what you're declining and why]
```

Use this for the kinds of refusals everyone puts off: declining a request you don't have time for, turning down a partnership that doesn't fit, saying no to a "quick coffee" with someone who's been pestering you.

---

## Step 4: Catch tone problems before sending

Before you hit send, paste your draft back in and ask:

```
Read this email as if you were the recipient. Tell me:
1. How does the tone come across? (warm, cold, defensive, passive-aggressive?)
2. Is there any sentence that could be misread?
3. What's the one thing you'd change?

Do not rewrite the email. Just answer.
```

This catches the email that *feels* fine to you but reads as cold to someone having a bad day. It has saved me from several disasters.

---

## What AI gets wrong

Be honest about this — AI is not magic.

- **It over-explains.** Three sentences become six. You have to cut.
- **It adds emotional padding** you didn't ask for ("I completely understand how frustrating this must be…"). Delete it.
- **It hallucinates context.** If you mention "the project," it might invent details. Always read before sending.
- **It defaults to American English.** If you write British English, or your second language is involved, tell it explicitly.
- **It cannot read your relationship with the person.** A note that's perfect for a stranger is wrong for your sister-in-law.

Rule of thumb: **AI writes the draft. You write the final sentence.** The last line of every email should still be in your own words.

## Homework

1. Collect 3 emails you wrote recently (real ones — sent from your account). Use the Step 1 prompt to generate your personal style guide. Save it in a notes app where you can find it again.
2. Pick one email you've been avoiding writing this week (a refusal, a follow-up, an awkward reply). Use the Step 2 prompt with your style guide. Send it.
3. Take an email you already sent and run it through the Step 4 "tone check." Note one thing you would change next time.
4. Optional: try the same rough note in both [claude.ai](https://claude.ai) and [chatgpt.com](https://chatgpt.com). Decide which one sounds more like you.
