# The AI Landscape: What's Out There Today

Before we talk about which AI to use, let's get a clear picture of what actually exists. Most people only know one name — usually ChatGPT — and assume that's the whole field. It isn't. There are four main general-purpose assistants, plus a long list of specialized tools. Knowing the map keeps you from forcing the wrong tool to do a job.

---

## The Four Big General Assistants

These are the ones you should at least recognize. They all do roughly the same thing — answer questions, write text, summarize, help with code — but they come from different companies with different strengths.

**Claude** — made by Anthropic. Lives at https://claude.ai. Strong at writing, reasoning, long documents, and following careful instructions. Free tier works fine for most daily use.

**ChatGPT** — made by OpenAI. Lives at https://chatgpt.com. The most well-known. Has the biggest ecosystem (custom GPTs, voice mode, image generation built in). Free tier is solid.

**Gemini** — made by Google. Lives at https://gemini.google.com. Tightly connected to Gmail, Google Docs, YouTube, and Google Search. If you live in Google's world, this matters.

**Copilot** — made by Microsoft (running OpenAI's models underneath). Lives at https://copilot.microsoft.com. Built into Windows, Word, Excel, Outlook. If your workplace runs on Microsoft 365, you may already have it.

A useful rule: pick one as your daily driver, know one other as a backup. Don't try to use all four.

---

## Beyond Chatbots

The chatbot is just one shape of AI tool. Here are the other categories worth knowing exist:

- **Image generators** — Midjourney, DALL-E (inside ChatGPT), Google's Nano Banana, Stable Diffusion. Type words, get pictures.
- **Transcription** — Otter.ai, Whisper, Fireflies. Turn audio or meetings into searchable text.
- **AI search engines** — Perplexity (https://perplexity.ai), You.com. Like Google, but the answer is written for you with sources cited.
- **Writing assistants** — Grammarly, Notion AI. Live inside the document you're already writing.
- **Voice and video** — ElevenLabs (voice cloning), HeyGen, Synthesia (AI avatars), Descript (podcast editing).
- **Coding assistants** — GitHub Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code. For people who write software.
- **Workflow tools** — Zapier AI, Make. Connect AI to other apps automatically.

You don't need to know all of these today. You need to know they exist so when somebody says "AI can't do X," you have a place to check.

## Three Quick Examples — Same Question, Different Tool

**"I need a transcript of yesterday's meeting so I can write up notes."**
Wrong tool: Claude or ChatGPT — they can't listen to audio recordings easily.
Right tool: Otter.ai or Whisper. Upload the recording, get text in minutes.

**"I need to find recent studies on a topic, with sources I can actually cite."**
Wrong tool: ChatGPT free version — older free models sometimes invent citations.
Right tool: Perplexity. It searches the live web and shows you the actual source links.

**"I need a visual concept — a logo, a poster, a product mockup."**
Wrong tool: A general chatbot — it will describe an image, not draw one.
Right tool: Midjourney or DALL-E. Type the description, get visual options.

The lesson here is not "memorize the tool list." It's "before you fight with a chatbot, ask yourself if the job belongs to a different tool entirely."

## What "AI Model" vs "AI Product" Means

One thing that confuses people: Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini are products. Inside them are models with version numbers like Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5, or Gemini 2.5. The product is the website or app you use. The model is the engine underneath. Companies release new models every few months. The product name usually stays the same; the engine gets swapped.

Practical effect: if somebody says "ChatGPT got smarter," they probably mean OpenAI shipped a new model behind the same website. You don't need to install anything.

---

## Free vs Paid — The Short Version

Every major assistant has a free tier and a paid tier (usually around $20/month).

Free is enough for: occasional questions, drafting emails, summarizing a few pages.

Paid gets you: the smartest version of the model, higher message limits, file uploads, image generation included, longer conversations before it forgets.

Honest take: if you use AI more than three times a week for real work, one paid subscription is worth it. Don't pay for two. Pick the one that fits your daily workflow.
