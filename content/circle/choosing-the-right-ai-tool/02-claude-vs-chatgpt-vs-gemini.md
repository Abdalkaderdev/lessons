# Claude vs ChatGPT vs Gemini: When to Use Which

You don't need three subscriptions. You need to know which one to reach for when the question lands on your desk. This lesson is a direct comparison, with honest weaknesses included.

---

## Claude — The Careful Writer

**Made by:** Anthropic
**Website:** https://claude.ai
**Free tier:** Yes — limited messages per day, resets every few hours.
**Paid tier:** Claude Pro, about $20/month. Higher limits, access to the smartest model.

**Strong at:**
- Long-form writing that sounds human, not robotic.
- Reading and summarizing long documents (you can paste 100+ pages).
- Following multi-step instructions without losing track.
- Reasoning carefully through messy problems.

**Weak at:**
- No built-in image generation — it can describe an image, not make one.
- No live web search on the free tier (limited search on paid).
- Smaller ecosystem — no "GPT store" equivalent.
- Will sometimes refuse tasks it thinks are risky, even when they aren't.

---

## ChatGPT — The All-Rounder

**Made by:** OpenAI
**Website:** https://chatgpt.com
**Free tier:** Yes — generous, but the smartest models are rate-limited.
**Paid tier:** ChatGPT Plus, about $20/month. Includes image generation, voice mode, file uploads, custom GPTs.

**Strong at:**
- Image generation built in (DALL-E).
- Voice conversations — you can literally talk to it.
- Huge library of "Custom GPTs" — pre-built assistants for niche tasks.
- Code generation and debugging.

**Weak at:**
- Writing can feel generic and over-polished without good prompting.
- Free tier sometimes invents facts confidently.
- The interface gets cluttered with features.

---

## Gemini — The Google Native

**Made by:** Google
**Website:** https://gemini.google.com
**Free tier:** Yes — solid, especially if you have a Google account.
**Paid tier:** Google AI Pro, about $20/month. Adds access to Gemini inside Gmail, Docs, and Drive.

**Strong at:**
- Lives inside Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Drive — pulls from your own files.
- Real-time Google Search integration — accurate on recent events.
- Strong with YouTube — can summarize videos directly from a link.
- Generous free tier.

**Weak at:**
- Writing style can feel flat compared to Claude.
- Less consistent reasoning on multi-step problems.
- Privacy concerns for some — your data is in Google's world.

---

## The Quick Decision Rule

- **Writing something important?** Claude.
- **Need an image right now?** ChatGPT.
- **Question about something that happened this week?** Gemini or ChatGPT (both have web search).
- **Working inside Gmail or Google Docs?** Gemini.
- **Working inside Word or Excel?** Copilot (or paste into Claude/ChatGPT).

Pick one as your daily driver. Use a second one only when the first hits a wall.

## Three Real Examples

**For the teacher** — "Write me a parent newsletter explaining the new grading system, warm but professional, about 300 words."

Try this prompt in all three:
```
You are helping me write a parent newsletter for my Year 8 class. The school is moving from letter grades to a 1-4 mastery scale. Write a 300-word message that is warm, professional, and reassures parents this is not "easier" grading. End with one sentence inviting questions.
```
In practice: Claude usually wins here — the tone is the most natural. ChatGPT is fine but reads more "corporate." Gemini tends to be shorter and flatter.

---

**For the NGO worker** — "I need to draft a 2-page concept note for a donor about a youth literacy program. I have rough notes."

```
I am drafting a 2-page concept note for a private foundation. The program is a youth literacy initiative for ages 9-14 in a region with limited library access. Below are my rough notes — turn them into a structured concept note with sections: Problem, Approach, Expected Outcomes, Budget Range, Next Steps. Keep the language clear and avoid jargon.

[paste rough notes here]
```
In practice: Claude is the strongest at long, structured documents. ChatGPT is a close second. Use Gemini if you also need it to pull in recent statistics from the web.

---

**For the business owner** — "I have a customer complaint email. Write a reply that takes responsibility without admitting legal fault, and offers a 20% credit."

```
A customer emailed complaining that their order arrived 5 days late and one item was damaged. Write a reply that:
- Acknowledges the problem clearly
- Apologizes without using corporate jargon
- Offers a 20% credit on their next order
- Does NOT admit fault in a way that creates liability
- Ends with a real human signoff, not "Best regards"

Customer email below:
[paste email]
```
In practice: Claude handles the "careful tone" instruction best. ChatGPT works but tends to slip into over-apologetic phrasing. Copilot is decent if you live in Outlook and want to reply in place.

## What About Pricing — Should You Pay?

Free tiers are genuinely useful in 2026. You can run a small business or write lessons on free Claude or free ChatGPT for weeks at a time. You will hit message limits if you use it for hours every day.

If you decide to pay, pay for one. Pick by your daily workflow:
- Live in Gmail/Docs all day → Gemini paid.
- Live in Word/Outlook all day → Copilot via Microsoft 365.
- You write and reason a lot → Claude Pro.
- You want everything in one tool including images → ChatGPT Plus.

Don't pay $60/month for three. That's the trap.

---

## Honest Warnings

- All three still make mistakes. Verify any fact, name, date, or citation before you use it publicly.
- Don't paste confidential client data, salaries, or medical info into the free versions — assume it could be used for training unless you've turned that off in settings.
- The "best" model changes every few months. Don't religiously stick to one — try the others every quarter.
