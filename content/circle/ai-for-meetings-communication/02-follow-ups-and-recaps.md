# Follow-Ups and Recaps

The meeting is the easy part. The follow-up is where most things die.

You meant to send the recap. You meant to email the parent. You meant to confirm the deadline. Three days pass. Now you have to write a long apologetic message instead of a short clean one.

AI does not solve discipline. But it removes the friction that makes follow-up feel heavy.

---

## The shape of a good follow-up

A follow-up message has four parts:

1. Thank you for the time
2. What we agreed on
3. What I will do, and by when
4. What I need from you, and by when

That is it. No padding. People skim follow-ups; they do not read them.

---

## Drafting from notes

You have a transcript or summary from the last lesson. Now you turn it into a message.

Open Claude (https://claude.ai) or ChatGPT (https://chat.openai.com) and use this:

```
Below are my notes from a meeting. Draft a follow-up email to [name].

Tone: [warm and direct / professional / friendly]
Length: under 150 words
Include: what was agreed, my next steps with dates, what I need from them with a date
Do not include: filler phrases, "I hope this email finds you well", or anything I cannot back up

Notes:
[paste notes here]
```

The "do not include" line matters. AI will pad an email with empty politeness by default. You have to tell it not to.

## Three flavors

Teacher — follow-up to a parent after the conference:

```
Draft a follow-up to a parent after a parent-teacher meeting about 
their daughter's reading progress. Warm but direct. Mention: the 
weekly reading log we agreed on, the book I will send home Friday, 
and ask them to sign the log each week. Under 120 words.
```

NGO assistant — follow-up to a team after a planning meeting:

```
Draft a team email recapping yesterday's planning meeting for the 
winter aid distribution. List who is doing what with deadlines. 
Flag the two open questions waiting on the director. Friendly, 
direct, under 200 words.
```

Business — follow-up to a prospect after a sales call:

```
Draft a follow-up to a prospect after a discovery call. Confirm 
their three stated pain points, the demo I will send by Thursday, 
and ask for a 30-minute follow-up next week. Professional, no 
buzzwords, under 150 words.
```

## Recap messages to people who were not there

A recap is different from a follow-up. A follow-up is to the people in the meeting. A recap is to people who missed it but need to know what happened.

```
Write a recap of this meeting for [person who was not present].
They care about: [what they care about].
They do not need: blow-by-blow detail.
Format: 3 short paragraphs, plain language, no jargon.
```

For the teacher, this is the email to the principal after a difficult parent meeting. For the NGO assistant, it is the message to the field officer who is on the road. For the business owner, it is the update to your partner who skipped the call.

## Thank-you notes

Short, specific, sent within 24 hours. AI is good for this because the hard part is starting.

```
Write a short thank-you message to [name] for [specific thing they did].
Mention one specific detail from our conversation so it does not feel 
generic. 3 sentences. Warm but not gushing.
```

The "specific detail" rule is what separates a real thank-you from a form letter.

---

## Holding people accountable, politely

This is the awkward one. Someone agreed to do something. They have not done it. You need to ask without sounding like you are nagging.

The trick is to make it easy for them to recover their dignity.

```
Draft a polite check-in to [name] about [task] they agreed to do by 
[date]. The deadline has passed. Tone: assume good faith, no guilt-trip, 
make it easy for them to respond quickly. Offer to help if there's a 
blocker. Under 80 words.
```

A good check-in says: "I am following up on X. Is there anything I can do to help unblock you?" Not: "You said you would do X and you have not done it."

- Teacher: the parent who said they would sign the homework folder and has not.
- NGO assistant: the volunteer who said they would send the photos and has not.
- Business: the client who said they would send the signed contract and has not.

Same structure, different stakes.

## Templating recurring follow-ups

If you send the same kind of message every week — Monday team recap, post-class parent note, weekly client update — build a template once and reuse it.

```
I send a [type] message every [frequency] to [audience]. 
Here is an example I sent last week: [paste]
Here is the rough content for this week: [paste]

Adapt my example into this week's version. Keep the same tone, 
structure, and length.
```

You write the first one yourself. AI handles the next fifty.

---

## What to watch out for

- AI loves the word "moreover." Strike it.
- AI defaults to "I hope this email finds you well." Strike that too.
- AI will invent details if your notes are thin. Read every draft before sending. If it says you promised a 3pm delivery and you did not, that is on you, not the model.
- Never send an AI draft to someone in distress — a grieving parent, a beneficiary in crisis — without rewriting it in your own voice. People can feel the difference.
