# The Phone Apps — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini

Most of us first met these tools through a browser tab on a laptop. That works, but it leaves the best half of these tools on the table. The phone is where AI stops being a website and starts being a thing you talk to, point at the world, and pull out of your pocket at a traffic light.

This lesson is about the three apps worth installing today, and what each one does that the web version can't.

---

## Why the apps matter

A browser tab can only read what you type. A phone has a microphone, a camera, a lock-screen shortcut, and a notification you can swipe down to. The apps wrap the same AI models in those phone features.

Three differences you feel immediately:

- **Voice.** You can hold the app like a phone call and just talk.
- **Camera.** You can point at something and ask about it.
- **Always there.** Lock screen, widget, share sheet — no opening a browser, no typing a URL.

---

## Claude

Download links:

- iOS and Android: https://claude.ai/download

Sign in with the same email you use on claude.ai. Conversations sync — start a chat on your laptop, finish it on the bus.

What the Claude app gives you over the browser:

- Voice input in the message bar (tap the mic).
- A share sheet entry — from any other app, hit Share and pick Claude to send a screenshot, a page, or selected text straight into a new conversation.
- Camera and photo attachments without dragging files around.

What it does not have (yet): a phone-call style voice mode like ChatGPT or Gemini Live. If "talk to it like a person" is the goal, Claude is not the one.

---

## ChatGPT

Download:

- App and details: https://openai.com/chatgpt
- Or search "ChatGPT" by OpenAI in your app store.

Sign in with the same account you use on chat.openai.com. Conversations, custom instructions, and memory carry across devices.

What the ChatGPT app adds:

- **Voice Mode.** Tap the headphones icon and have a back-and-forth conversation. It interrupts, you interrupt, it sounds close to a phone call. This is genuinely the feature that changes how people use AI.
- **Widgets.** On iOS and Android, drop a ChatGPT widget on the home screen. One tap and you're typing.
- **Lock-screen shortcut on iOS.** Add the ChatGPT action to Control Center or the action button (iPhone 15 Pro and up) so a single press opens voice mode.
- **Share sheet** for sending screenshots, articles, and images directly into a chat.

## Gemini

Download:

- Android: search "Google" — Gemini lives inside the Google app, or install the standalone Gemini app.
- iOS: install the Gemini app, or use it inside the main Google app.
- Web: https://gemini.google.com

On Pixel and recent Samsung phones, Gemini can replace Google Assistant as the default phone-wide assistant. Long-press the power button (or say "Hey Google") and Gemini answers instead of the old Assistant.

What that unlocks:

- Ask anything hands-free without opening an app.
- "Gemini Live" — a conversational voice mode similar to ChatGPT Voice Mode.
- Tight hooks into Gmail, Calendar, Maps, and YouTube if you let it connect.

The trade-off: making it your default assistant means it sees a lot. Read the permission screen before you tap through.

---

## Logging in once, using it everywhere

A small thing that saves real friction: log in on the phone app with the exact same account as the web. Then:

- A chat you start on the laptop appears on the phone within seconds.
- Custom instructions and saved context follow you.
- You can dictate a rough idea on a walk and finish editing it on a real keyboard later.

Do not make a new account for the phone. People do this and end up with two split conversation histories and no idea where anything is.

---

## When the phone is better than the browser

- You want to talk, not type.
- You want to point a camera at something.
- You're walking, driving (hands-free), cooking, or in a waiting room.
- You want to forward a message, screenshot, or photo into a chat in one tap.
- You want a quick answer without opening a laptop.

When the browser is still better:

- Long writing sessions where you need a real keyboard.
- Pasting and editing large blocks of code or text.
- Side-by-side reference across multiple tabs.

---

## Honest caveats

- The apps crash. Voice mode drops calls. Conversations sometimes fail to sync for a few minutes.
- Voice transcription mishears names, foreign words, and anything in a noisy room.
- The "default assistant" swap on Android occasionally breaks until you reboot.
- Free tiers have lower limits than the paid plans on the same apps. If voice mode stops working, you may have hit your cap.

None of this is a reason to avoid the apps. It is a reason to not be surprised when they misbehave.

---

## A quick test prompt

Once installed, open any of the three, tap the mic, and say this out loud:

```
Give me three things I should try on the phone version of this app that I can't easily do in the browser. Keep each one to one sentence.
```

If the answer feels useful, you're in. If voice didn't pick up your words, check microphone permission in your phone's settings.

## Homework

1. Install all three apps (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) and sign in with the same accounts you already use on the web.
2. On ChatGPT, open Voice Mode and have a 60-second back-and-forth about what you ate today. Notice what it gets right and what it mishears.
3. On Android, try setting Gemini as your default assistant for one day. If you hate it, switch back — write down one thing that worked and one thing that annoyed you.
4. Add a ChatGPT or Claude widget to your home screen, or pin one to your iPhone Control Center, so opening it is a single tap.
