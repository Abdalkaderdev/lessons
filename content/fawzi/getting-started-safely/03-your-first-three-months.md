The difference between traders who survive year one and traders who blow up usually comes down to the first 90 days. This is a slow, deliberate plan: paper, then tiny real size, then small scale-up only if the numbers are good. **Most retail traders never become consistently profitable** — your only job in month one is to make sure you are not one of them.

---

## The 90-day plan

| Month | Capital at risk | Focus | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Month 1** | $0 (paper only) | Learn one strategy deeply | No real money. None. |
| **Month 2** | Smallest possible size (~$100 max risk per week) | Translate paper skill to real emotion | Position size stays fixed |
| **Month 3** | Review and decide | Refine, or stop | Scale up only with proof |

---

## Month 1 — Paper trade, one strategy only

Pick **one** setup. Just one. Examples:

- Pullback to 20 EMA in an uptrend
- Breakout from a multi-week range
- Mean reversion on RSI extremes in a sideways market

Trade only that setup on TradingView paper trading. Log every trade. Aim for 20 to 30 paper trades this month.

**Warning:** The temptation to trade five strategies at once is enormous. Resist it. You cannot improve at something you only do four times a month.

End of month checklist:

- 20+ paper trades logged
- Win rate and average R calculated
- Strategy expectancy is positive
- You can describe the setup in one sentence

If any of these fail, **repeat month 1**. Do not advance.

---

## Month 2 — Real money, tiny size

Switch to real money with a position size so small that losing it does not affect your week. A practical rule:

> **Maximum risk per trade = 0.5% of total account.**
> **Maximum risk per week = 2% of total account.**

If you funded with $2,000, that is $10 risk per trade and $40 risk per week. Yes, the dollar profits will be embarrassing. That is the point — you are buying experience, not income.

What you are testing in month 2 is not the strategy (you already proved it on paper). You are testing **yourself**:

- Do you still follow your stop loss when real money is on the line?
- Do you take valid setups, or do you freeze?
- Do you over-trade out of boredom?

Real money triggers emotions paper never does. That is the entire reason for this month.

---

## Month 3 — Review, refine, decide

Sit down with your full journal. Compare paper trading (month 1) to live trading (month 2). The questions:

- Is my live win rate within 10% of my paper win rate? (If not, emotion is changing my execution.)
- Did I follow my rules on 90%+ of trades?
- Am I net profitable, breakeven, or down?

Then decide:

| Result after month 2 | Action for month 3 |
|---|---|
| Profitable AND followed rules 90%+ | Scale position size by 25%, not 100% |
| Breakeven, rules followed | Hold size, refine entries, keep trading |
| Losing, rules followed | Go back to paper. Strategy needs work. |
| Losing, rules broken | Stop trading. Fix discipline first. |

---

## The #1 way beginners blow up

After a loss, the brain screams **"trade bigger to win it back."** This is called revenge trading or "doubling down," and it is the single most common reason retail accounts go to zero.

A typical sequence:

1. Lose $40 (your planned risk).
2. Feel angry / embarrassed.
3. Next trade: risk $80 to "make it back fast."
4. Lose $80.
5. Now risk $200.
6. Account down 30% in three days.

**The rule:** Position size never increases after a loss. If anything, it decreases. You only scale up after a defined run of profitable, rule-following weeks — never in reaction to a single trade.

## Use AI to...

End-of-month review. Paste your full month's journal:

```text
You are my trading performance coach. Below is my complete trade journal for the past month
(month [1/2/3] of my plan).

Analyze and tell me:
1. Expectancy per trade (in R) and total R for the month.
2. My best setup and my worst setup, by R generated.
3. Three specific behavioral mistakes I made repeatedly.
4. Whether my live execution matches my paper execution (if I have both).
5. Based on the 90-day plan rules, what should I do next month:
   stay at this stage, advance, scale up, or stop?

Be direct. I want the truth, not encouragement.

[paste journal here]
```

## Homework

1. Write your one strategy on a single page: entry rules, stop placement, target, position size formula. Tape it next to your monitor.
2. Calculate your 0.5% per-trade and 2% per-week dollar risk based on your real funded account size. Write both numbers in your journal header.
3. Schedule a recurring calendar event on the last day of each month called "90-day plan review" and run the AI review prompt above on that day.
