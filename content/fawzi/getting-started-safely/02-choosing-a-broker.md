Your broker is the door between you and the market. A bad broker can quietly cost you 20% a year in spreads, slippage, and fees, or worse, freeze your account when you need to exit. Pick once, pick carefully, and prioritize **regulation over flashy features**.

---

## What actually matters

Ignore the marketing. Five things matter:

1. **Regulation** in a country with a real legal system (SEC/FINRA in US, FCA in UK, BaFin in Germany, ASIC in Australia, FSRA/DFSA in UAE)
2. **Low fees** — commission per trade, spreads, overnight financing, withdrawal fees
3. **Execution speed and quality** — does your order fill at the price you saw?
4. **Platform quality** — usable on desktop and mobile, integrates with TradingView if possible
5. **Asset access** — can you trade what your strategy requires?

Everything else (welcome bonuses, copy trading, "social" features) is either irrelevant or a red flag.

**Note for MENA users:** verify your country is on the broker's accepted list before opening. Rules change. Some brokers (eToro, Plus500) operate locally but use CFDs, which are far riskier than spot.

## Red flags — close the tab immediately

Avoid any broker that offers:

- **Leverage above 30:1** on forex or 5:1 on stocks for retail accounts. EU and US regulators capped these for a reason. Brokers offering 500:1 are targeting people they expect to lose.
- **Deposit bonuses** ("get $500 free when you deposit $1000"). Always come with withdrawal conditions designed to trap your capital.
- **No regulator listed** or vague "international license" from Saint Vincent, Vanuatu, or the Marshall Islands.
- **You are their counterparty** (market makers / B-book brokers) without clear disclosure. They profit when you lose.
- **No segregated client funds.** If the broker goes bust, your money should be untouchable.
- **Aggressive sales calls** after signup pushing you to deposit more.

**Warning:** If a broker is advertised heavily on Instagram or YouTube by influencers with Lamborghinis, assume it is in the second list, not the first.

## How to verify regulation

Take 5 minutes:

1. Find the broker's claimed regulator (e.g., "FCA-regulated, firm reference 208159")
2. Go to the regulator's official register (FCA: register.fca.org.uk, SEC: brokercheck.finra.org)
3. Search the firm number. If it does not appear, or shows warnings, walk away.

## Use AI to...

Compare two brokers you are considering:

```text
I am a retail trader in [your country] with about $[amount] to fund my first account.
I want to trade [stocks / forex / crypto / futures] using a swing strategy holding 1-5 days.

Compare these two brokers for my use case:
1. [Broker A]
2. [Broker B]

For each, give me:
- Regulator and jurisdiction (verify the regulator is real)
- Commission per trade for my asset class
- Typical spread/slippage reputation
- Minimum deposit
- Withdrawal fees and speed
- Known red flags or recent regulatory actions
- TradingView integration: yes/no

End with a one-sentence recommendation and why.
```

Then **verify the regulator yourself** on the official register. Do not skip this step.
