# Stop Losses and Risk/Reward

A stop loss is a pre-decided price at which you exit a losing trade automatically. It is the seatbelt of trading. Without one, a small loss becomes a catastrophic loss the moment the market moves against you and you start "hoping."

**A trade without a stop loss is not a trade — it is a prayer.** Most beginners do not lose because they pick bad trades. They lose because they refuse to accept a small loss and let it turn into an account-ending loss.

---

## The Only Two Acceptable Stop Movements

You may move your stop loss **only in one direction: toward reducing risk.**

| Action | Acceptable? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Move stop to breakeven once trade is profitable | YES | Locks in zero risk, trade is now "free" |
| Trail stop up as price rises (long trade) | YES | Protects accumulated profit |
| Move stop further away to "give it room" | **NEVER** | This is how accounts die |
| Cancel the stop entirely "just for now" | **NEVER** | Pure denial — guaranteed disaster |

The moment a trader moves a stop further from price, they have admitted the trade is wrong but are unwilling to take the loss. The market does not care about your feelings. **Take the small loss. Always.**

---

## Risk/Reward Ratio (R:R)

R:R is the comparison of how much you can lose vs. how much you can win.

```
R:R = (Target - Entry) / (Entry - Stop)
```

| Setup | Risk | Reward | R:R |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry $100, Stop $98, Target $102 | $2 | $2 | 1:1 (bad) |
| Entry $100, Stop $98, Target $104 | $2 | $4 | 1:2 (minimum) |
| Entry $100, Stop $98, Target $106 | $2 | $6 | 1:3 (great) |
| Entry $100, Stop $98, Target $110 | $2 | $10 | 1:5 (rare gold) |

**Minimum rule: never take a trade with less than 1:2 risk/reward.**

---

## Why a 40% Win Rate Still Prints Money

Beginners obsess over win rate. Professionals obsess over R:R. Watch what happens with 10 trades at $10 risk each:

| Win Rate | R:R | Wins | Losses | P/L |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 70% | 1:1 | 7 x $10 = $70 | 3 x $10 = $30 | +$40 |
| 40% | 1:3 | 4 x $30 = $120 | 6 x $10 = $60 | **+$60** |
| 30% | 1:5 | 3 x $50 = $150 | 7 x $10 = $70 | **+$80** |

The trader with the *worst* win rate (30%) made the *most* money. **You can be wrong more often than right and still get rich — if your winners are bigger than your losers.**

This is why "I was right!" is not a useful sentence in trading. Being right small and wrong big is how you go broke being right.

## Use AI to...

Pressure-test every setup before clicking buy:

```text
I am considering a trade. Entry: $___, Stop: $___, Target: $___. Calculate: (1) the risk in dollars per unit, (2) the reward in dollars per unit, (3) the exact R:R ratio. If the R:R is less than 1:2, tell me to SKIP this trade and explain why. Do not be polite — be blunt.
```

```text
Act as my trade journal coach. I just closed a trade. Ask me: Did I hit my original stop, or did I move it? If I moved the stop further away from entry, scold me and remind me of the 1% rule. Then ask what I will do differently next time.
```

## Homework

1. Pull up the last 5 trades you took (real or paper). For each, write down the original stop and whether you moved it. Notice the pattern.
2. Build a simple table of 10 hypothetical trades with a 40% win rate at 1:3 R:R. Confirm with math that the account grows even with 6 losses.
3. Set up automatic stop-loss orders on your broker/exchange. Practice placing the stop *before* placing the entry. Stop first, entry second — always.
