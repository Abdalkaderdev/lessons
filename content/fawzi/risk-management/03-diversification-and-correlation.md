# Diversification and Correlation

Diversification means spreading your money across different trades so one bad event cannot destroy you. But here is the trap most beginners fall into: **owning five things that all move together is not diversification — it is one big trade in disguise.**

If you are long Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, and a crypto-mining stock, you do not have four trades. You have one trade that the market calls "crypto goes up." When crypto crashes (and it will), all four positions lose at the same time.

---

## What Correlation Means

Correlation measures how two assets move relative to each other, on a scale from -1 to +1.

| Correlation | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| +1.0 | Move identically | BTC and ETH (most days) |
| +0.7 to +0.9 | Move together strongly | Tech stocks: AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL |
| 0 | No relationship | Bitcoin and oats futures |
| -0.7 to -1.0 | Move opposite | Stocks vs. VIX (fear index) |

**Two highly correlated trades = one trade with double the risk.** If you risk 1% on BTC and 1% on ETH, on a bad day you do not lose 1% — you lose closer to 2%.

---

## The Hidden Cluster Risk

Here is a portfolio that *looks* diversified but is secretly one bet:

| Position | "Category" | Real Driver |
|---|---|---|
| Tesla (TSLA) | US Tech Stock | Risk-on appetite |
| Bitcoin (BTC) | Crypto | Risk-on appetite |
| ARKK ETF | Innovation ETF | Risk-on appetite |
| NVDA call options | Options | Risk-on appetite |

All four crash on the same day when the Fed raises rates. The trader thought they had four ideas — they had one idea bet four times. **A bad correlation audit can turn a 1% trade plan into a 4% account drawdown overnight.**

---

## Real Diversification

Real diversification spreads risk across assets that respond to *different* economic forces.

| Bucket | Examples | Reacts To |
|---|---|---|
| Equities | SPY, QQQ | Growth, earnings |
| Crypto | BTC, ETH | Liquidity, risk appetite |
| Commodities | Gold, oil | Inflation, supply shocks |
| Bonds / Cash | T-bills, USD | Safety, interest rates |

A simple rule: **never have more than 2-3% of your account at risk in any single correlation cluster at once.** If BTC and ETH are both open, count them as one trade for risk purposes.

---

## The Danger of Overleverage

Leverage multiplies both your position and your risk. A 10x leveraged position moves 10x faster — in both directions. A 5% adverse move = 50% account gone.

Combine leverage with correlated positions and you have built a financial suicide machine:

| Setup | Real Exposure | Risk if Cluster Drops 5% |
|---|---|---|
| 3 crypto longs, 1x leverage | 3x | -15% account |
| 3 crypto longs, 5x leverage | 15x | **-75% account** |
| 3 crypto longs, 10x leverage | 30x | **Account liquidated** |

**Most account blow-ups are not from one bad trade. They are from three correlated bad trades hitting at once while leveraged.**

## Use AI to...

Audit your portfolio for hidden correlation before adding a new trade:

```text
I currently hold these open positions: [list each ticker and direction, e.g., "long BTC, long ETH, long TSLA"]. I am considering adding [new ticker]. Analyze: (1) how correlated each of my positions is to the others, (2) what single economic event would hurt all of them at once, (3) whether adding the new ticker increases or decreases my real diversification. Be honest — if I am just adding another bet on the same theme, say so.
```

```text
Calculate my real cluster risk. Account = $___. Current open positions and risk per trade: [list]. Group them by correlation cluster (crypto, US tech, commodities, etc.) and tell me my total % at risk per cluster. Flag any cluster above 3%.
```

## Homework

1. List every open position you have right now. Group them by what economic force drives them. Be honest about hidden correlations.
2. Calculate your total combined risk if every "risk-on" position lost at the same time. Is it more than 3% of your account? If yes, close or reduce something today.
3. Pick one trade you would normally take and find an *uncorrelated* second idea instead of doubling down on the same theme. Note which feels harder — that resistance is the bias you need to defeat.
