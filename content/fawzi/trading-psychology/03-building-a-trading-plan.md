# Building a Trading Plan

A written trading plan is the cheapest, most underused defense against your own brain. When the market is moving and your heart is pounding, you will not invent good decisions on the fly — you will execute whatever default behavior is already wired in. The plan replaces that default with something you wrote calmly, in advance, when nobody's money was on the line.

---

## Why writing it down matters

A plan in your head is not a plan. It's a feeling, and feelings shift the second the candle moves against you. A plan on paper — that you read out loud before every session — is a contract with the calm version of you. The version under stress has to honor it.

> If it isn't written down, it doesn't exist. If it isn't reviewed before the session, it doesn't matter.

---

## What goes in the plan

A real trading plan covers six things. Anything less and you're freelancing.

**1. Market conditions you trade**

- What instruments? (e.g., BTC/ETH only, or specific stocks)
- What time of day? (e.g., London open, NY session)
- What regime? (trending, ranging — and how you tell the difference)
- What conditions make you sit out? (low volume, major news, holidays)

**2. Entry rules**

- Exact setup criteria — what must be true on the chart before you click buy
- Required confirmation (volume? close above level? second candle?)
- The setup must be objective enough that two people looking at the same chart would agree it's there

**3. Exit rules**

- Stop loss — defined before entry, based on chart structure, not on how much you're willing to lose
- Profit target(s) — fixed R-multiple, or trailing rule
- Time stop — if the trade hasn't moved within X bars, exit

**4. Position size rules**

- Fixed % of account risked per trade (1% is standard; 0.5% if you're new)
- Position size = (account x risk %) / (entry - stop) — calculate it, don't eyeball it
- Never average down on a loser. Ever.

**5. Max daily and weekly loss limits**

- Daily stop: e.g., -3% account → close the platform for the day
- Weekly stop: e.g., -6% → done for the week, review only
- These are not suggestions. They're circuit breakers.

**6. When to stop trading for the day**

- After hitting daily loss limit
- After 2 consecutive losses (cooling-off rule)
- If you feel emotional, tired, angry, or excited
- After hitting daily profit target (yes — protect green days too)

> The trader who knows when to stop beats the trader who knows when to enter.

---

## Reviewing the plan before each session

Five minutes. Out loud. Every day. Before you open a single chart:

1. Read your plan top to bottom
2. State today's max loss number
3. State your "I'll stop trading if ___" rules
4. Confirm you're rested, not emotional, not chasing yesterday's result

If any of those fail, you don't trade. Cheap day off > expensive blow-up.

## Use AI to...

Stress-test your plan before you start trading it:

```text
Here is my written trading plan: [paste full plan]. Act as a strict trading mentor. Find every place where my rules are vague, contradictory, or exploitable by my own emotions. Point out missing risk controls. Tell me where I left wiggle room for myself. Rewrite weak sections.
```

End-of-day enforcement check:

```text
Here is my plan: [paste]. Here is what I actually did today: [paste trades, sizes, exits, emotional notes]. For each trade, tell me whether I followed my plan or broke it, and which specific rule. End with one sentence: "Plan adherence today: X/10."
```

---

## Homework

1. Write v1 of your plan using the template. It will be bad. That's fine — a bad written plan beats a perfect unwritten one. Save it where you'll see it tomorrow.
2. Tape your daily and weekly loss limits somewhere physical and visible. Numbers, not vibes.
3. For the next 10 sessions, read your plan out loud before trading and grade your adherence 1-10 at the end. Adjust the plan weekly based on where you keep breaking it — but never relax a risk rule after a losing day.
