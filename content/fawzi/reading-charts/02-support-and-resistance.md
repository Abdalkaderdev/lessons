# Support and Resistance

**Support** is a price level where buyers have stepped in before and pushed price back up. **Resistance** is a level where sellers have stepped in and pushed price back down. These are the most important lines on any chart — almost every trade idea starts with them.

---

## Why These Levels Exist

Price is just memory. When a stock bounced from $100 last month, traders remember. Next time it gets near $100, some people buy ("it bounced here before"), and that buying actually makes it bounce again. The level becomes real because enough people believe it is.

- **Support** = floor. Buyers wake up.
- **Resistance** = ceiling. Sellers wake up.

The more times a level has been tested without breaking, the **stronger** it is — until it breaks.

---

## How to Draw Them

You do not need to be perfect. Aim for zones, not exact prices.

1. Zoom out to the **daily or weekly** chart.
2. Look for **horizontal price areas** where the chart turned around at least twice.
3. Draw a horizontal line through the **bodies** of candles, not the wicks (wicks are noise).
4. Mark **3-5 levels max** per chart. More than that is clutter.

| Type | What to look for |
|------|------------------|
| **Strong** | Touched 3+ times, held each time |
| **Weak** | Touched once, never re-tested |
| **Fresh** | Just formed — unproven but worth watching |

---

## Round Numbers Matter

Humans love round numbers. **$100, $1,000, $50,000** — these act as psychological levels even if no chart history exists there. BTC at $100k is not magic; it is just where everyone has set alerts and orders.

Treat **round numbers** as automatic support or resistance until proven otherwise.

---

## Breakouts and Breakdowns

When price punches through a level, something changes.

- **Breakout** = price closes **above resistance** with conviction. Often the start of a new leg up.
- **Breakdown** = price closes **below support**. Often the start of a drop.

What to watch for:

- A **strong close** beyond the level (not just a wick poking through)
- **Above-average volume** on the breakout candle
- A **retest** — price often comes back to the level before continuing

Fake breakouts are common. Wait for the close, not the touch.

---

## Role Reversal

This is the most useful concept in this whole lesson:

**Old resistance becomes new support. Old support becomes new resistance.**

When price breaks above a ceiling, that ceiling often becomes the new floor. Why? The traders who sold there now feel wrong — when price comes back, they buy to get back in. The level flips.

This is why a **retest of broken resistance** is one of the highest-probability entry zones in trading.
