# Economic Indicators That Move Markets

Individual stocks have their own stories, but the **macro tide** lifts or sinks all boats. A handful of economic releases each month can swing the entire market by 2-3% in minutes. Knowing what they are — and how to read the reaction — is non-negotiable.

---

## The Big Five Releases

| Indicator | What it measures | Frequency | Why it matters |
|-----------|------------------|-----------|----------------|
| **CPI** (Inflation) | Price changes for consumers | Monthly | Drives Fed policy |
| **Fed Rate Decision** | Cost of borrowing money | 8x per year | Affects every asset price |
| **NFP** (Non-Farm Payrolls) | New jobs created | First Friday monthly | Signals economic strength |
| **GDP** | Total economic output | Quarterly | Confirms expansion or recession |
| **Retail Sales** | Consumer spending | Monthly | Consumer = 70% of US economy |

---

## How Markets React

The trick: markets do not react to the **number** — they react to the **surprise** vs. expectations.

| Scenario | Typical reaction |
|----------|------------------|
| CPI **higher** than expected | Stocks fall, bond yields rise, dollar strengthens |
| CPI **lower** than expected | Stocks rally, yields fall, dollar weakens |
| NFP **stronger** than expected | Mixed — good economy but delays rate cuts |
| Fed **cuts rates** unexpectedly | Stocks rally hard, bonds rally |
| Fed signals **higher for longer** | Stocks sell off, especially tech |

---

## Why Bonds and Stocks Sometimes Move Oppositely

Bonds pay a fixed interest rate. When the Fed **raises rates**:

- New bonds pay more, so old bonds become less attractive — **bond prices fall**
- Borrowing gets expensive for companies, so future profits look smaller — **stocks fall**
- But sometimes stocks rally on "bad economic news" because traders expect the Fed to **cut rates sooner** — this is the "bad news is good news" reflex

Understanding this seesaw is the difference between panicking at a headline and seeing the opportunity.

## The Economic Calendar

You should check this every Sunday and every morning before US markets open:

- **Forex Factory** (`forexfactory.com/calendar`) — color-coded by impact (red = high), shows expected vs. actual side by side
- **Investing.com Economic Calendar** (`investing.com/economic-calendar`) — filterable by country, shows historical surprises
- **Trading Economics** — good for global macro and lesser-known indicators

Look at the **forecast** column. That is the consensus. Trade reactions are about the gap between forecast and actual.

---

## A Trader's Macro Checklist

Every Sunday night, ask yourself:

1. Is there a **Fed meeting** this week?
2. When does **CPI** drop?
3. Is **NFP Friday** this week?
4. Any **GDP** or **retail sales** prints?

If yes to any of these, expect volatility on that day and avoid opening new positions in the 30 minutes before the release.
