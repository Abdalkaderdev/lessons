# Reading Financial Statements

Every public company tells its story through three documents: the **income statement**, the **balance sheet**, and the **cash flow statement**. If you can read these three, you can judge whether a business is healthy, growing, and worth owning — without ever opening a chart.

---

## The Three Statements

| Statement | What it shows | Time frame |
|-----------|---------------|------------|
| **Income Statement** | Revenue minus expenses = profit | Period (quarter/year) |
| **Balance Sheet** | What the company owns vs. owes | Snapshot in time |
| **Cash Flow Statement** | Actual cash moving in and out | Period (quarter/year) |

Profits can be manipulated with accounting tricks. **Cash flow is harder to fake** — that is why experienced investors read it first.

---

## Key Numbers to Watch

| Metric | Why it matters | Healthy sign |
|--------|----------------|--------------|
| **Revenue growth** | Is the business getting bigger? | 10%+ YoY for growth stocks |
| **Gross margin** | How much profit per dollar of sales | Stable or rising |
| **Net income** | Bottom-line profit | Positive and growing |
| **Free cash flow (FCF)** | Cash left after running the business | Positive, growing |
| **Total debt** | What the company owes | Manageable vs. equity |

A company can post "record revenue" while burning cash. Always check **FCF** alongside net income.

---

## Where to Find the Reports

- **SEC EDGAR** (`sec.gov/edgar`) — official 10-K (annual) and 10-Q (quarterly) filings, free, unfiltered
- **Yahoo Finance** — quick view of financials under the "Financials" tab
- **Stockanalysis.com** — clean 10-year summary tables, ratios pre-calculated
- **The company's Investor Relations page** — earnings slides and management commentary

The **10-K** is the bible: audited, detailed, includes risk factors. The **10-Q** is the lighter quarterly update.

---

## The Three Ratios That Matter

| Ratio | Formula | What it tells you |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| **P/E** (Price/Earnings) | Stock price / earnings per share | How many years of profit you are paying for |
| **P/S** (Price/Sales) | Market cap / annual revenue | Useful when company is not yet profitable |
| **EV/EBITDA** | Enterprise value / EBITDA | Compares companies with different debt levels |

Rule of thumb: a **P/E of 15-25** is normal for a stable business. A **P/E of 60+** means the market expects huge growth — and punishes you if growth slows.

## Use AI to...

Paste a 10-K into Claude and ask:

```text
I'm pasting the management discussion section of a 10-K filing. In plain English, tell me:
1. What does this company actually sell?
2. How did revenue and free cash flow change vs. last year?
3. What are the top 3 risks management is worried about?
4. Are there any red flags in the language they use?
```

Or for ratio analysis:

```text
Here are 5 years of financials for [TICKER] from Stockanalysis.com. Calculate revenue CAGR, gross margin trend, and FCF growth. Tell me if this looks like a healthy compounder or a struggling business.
```
