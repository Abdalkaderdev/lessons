# News, Earnings, and Expectations

A stock is not a company — it is a **bet on future expectations**. That is why a company can announce record profits and the stock still drops 15% in after-hours trading. Once you understand the gap between news and expectations, the market stops feeling random.

---

## How News Moves Stocks

| News type | Typical impact |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Earnings beat + raised guidance** | Stock pops 5-15% |
| **Earnings beat, lowered guidance** | Stock often falls |
| **Analyst upgrade** | Mild pop, fades quickly |
| **Lawsuit, fraud, recall** | Sharp drop, slow recovery |
| **CEO resignation** | Depends on whether CEO was loved or blamed |
| **Acquisition (the buyer)** | Usually falls short-term |
| **Acquisition (the target)** | Pops to deal price |

The pattern: markets always look **forward**. Today's number is already priced in. **Tomorrow's outlook** is what trades.

---

## Earnings Season

Public US companies report **4 times per year**, roughly:

- **Mid-January to mid-February** — Q4 / full-year results
- **Mid-April to mid-May** — Q1
- **Mid-July to mid-August** — Q2
- **Mid-October to mid-November** — Q3

The biggest names (Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Amazon, Google, Meta, Tesla) cluster in the last two weeks of each window. These "mega-cap weeks" can move the entire S&P 500.

---

## What "Beating Estimates" Actually Means

Wall Street analysts publish **consensus estimates** for revenue and earnings per share (EPS) weeks before the report. The stock's reaction depends on three numbers:

| Number | Source |
|--------|--------|
| **Reported EPS** | The actual result the company posts |
| **Consensus EPS** | The average of analyst estimates |
| **Whisper number** | The unofficial buy-side expectation (often higher) |

A company can **beat the consensus** but **miss the whisper**, and the stock still falls. This is why "beat earnings, stock down" headlines are common and confusing to beginners.

---

## Why Great Earnings Can Crush a Stock

Three killers, in order of frequency:

1. **Weak forward guidance** — management projects slowing growth next quarter
2. **Sky-high expectations** — the stock had already rallied 30% into the print, so even a great number is "priced in"
3. **One bad metric in a great report** — like declining gross margins or a slowing key business segment

This is why traders say "buy the rumor, sell the news." The smart money positioned weeks ago. The earnings release is when they exit.

---

## How to Read an Earnings Report in 10 Minutes

Open the press release (PDF on the company's IR page) and scan in this order:

| Minute | What to find |
|--------|--------------|
| 0-2 | Headline EPS and revenue vs. consensus |
| 2-4 | **Forward guidance** for next quarter and full year |
| 4-6 | Revenue by segment — is the core business growing? |
| 6-8 | Gross margin and operating margin trend |
| 8-10 | CEO commentary in the opening paragraph (tone matters) |

Then check **Yahoo Finance** or **Stockanalysis.com** for the after-hours price reaction. The first 30 minutes of trading reveal the market's verdict.

## Use AI to...

Summarize an earnings report fast:

```text
I'm pasting a Q3 earnings press release for [TICKER]. In 5 bullets: (1) did they beat on revenue and EPS? (2) what is their guidance for next quarter? (3) which business segment grew fastest and which shrank? (4) what is the CEO's tone — confident, cautious, or defensive? (5) what is the single biggest risk hidden in the report?
```

Or to prep for an earnings trade:

```text
[TICKER] reports earnings tomorrow after the close. Tell me: the consensus EPS and revenue estimates, how the stock has moved into the last 4 earnings prints, the implied move from options pricing if available, and the top 3 things analysts are watching.
```
