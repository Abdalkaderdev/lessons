# Trading Terminology

Every field has its own slang, and trading has more than most. The good news: **the vocabulary is small once you've seen it twice**. This lesson is your reference sheet. Don't try to memorize it — just recognize it when you see it.

---

## Direction: Long and Short

- **Long** — you bought it and you profit if the price goes **up**. "I'm long Tesla" = "I own Tesla and want it to rise."
- **Short** — you borrowed it, sold it, and profit if the price goes **down**. "I'm short Tesla" = "I'm betting Tesla falls."

**Example:** You short 10 shares of TSLA at $250. Price drops to $230. You buy back at $230, return the shares, keep **$200 profit** ($20 × 10).

---

## Size and Borrowing: Position, Leverage, Margin

- **Position** — your current open trade. "I have a position in BTC" = "I'm currently holding BTC with the intent to close it later."
- **Leverage** — using borrowed money to control more than you own. **10x leverage** means $1,000 controls a $10,000 position.
- **Margin** — the deposit your broker requires as collateral when you use leverage. It's your skin in the game.

**Example:** You deposit $1,000 margin. Your broker gives you 5x leverage, so you can open a $5,000 position. If the asset moves 2% in your favor, you make $100 (10% of your $1,000). If it moves 2% against you, you lose $100. **Leverage multiplies both directions.**

---

## Units: Pip, Lot, Tick

These are the "inches and feet" of trading — how price and size are measured. Different markets, different units.

| Term | Where used | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pip** | Forex | The 4th decimal place of a price | EUR/USD goes 1.0850 → 1.0851 = 1 pip |
| **Lot** | Forex | A standardized trade size | 1 standard lot = 100,000 units of currency |
| **Tick** | Stocks, futures | The smallest possible price move | A stock ticks from $50.00 to $50.01 |

You don't need to master these on day one. Just know that when a forex trader says "I made 30 pips," they mean a 30-unit move in the 4th decimal.

---

## Market Health: Volume, Liquidity, Volatility

These three describe **how the market is behaving** right now. Read them like a doctor reads vital signs.

- **Volume** — how many shares/contracts traded in a period. **High volume = lots of activity = more reliable price moves.**
- **Liquidity** — how easily you can buy or sell without moving the price. Apple is highly liquid (millions of buyers). A tiny penny stock is illiquid (your order moves the market).
- **Volatility** — how wildly the price swings. **High volatility = bigger moves both ways = bigger opportunity and bigger risk.**

**Example:** Bitcoin on a quiet Sunday might move 0.5% in an hour (low volatility). Bitcoin during a Fed announcement might move 5% in an hour (high volatility). Same asset, different conditions.
